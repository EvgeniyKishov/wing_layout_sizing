FINE SIZING: COMPARISON OF FOUR SCREENED RIB-COUNT WINNERS
19 November 2026

The 2-rib layout is lightest among the four completed fixed-layout fine sizings: 483.136 kg, 65.370 kg (11.918%) below the 16-rib case.
This is a result under the adopted filtered-stress and linear-buckling criteria for one prescribed load case. It is not a global layout optimum or a physical-strength validation.

CONTROLLED SETUP
Two ribs (root and tip); spars at 17.5% and 70% chord. 23,552 shell elements, made by four-child subdivision of the standard 5,888-element mesh. Fresh uniform 10 mm start.
Source/input hashes and sizing settings match the saved 16-rib baseline: R=0.30 m, full-integration SHELL181, original 3/3/1 layer quadrature and offsets, direct FSD, grow-only buckling correction, early restoration, SMP4.
Same aerodynamic source and conservative load transfer. The 8-, 16- and 25-rib results were reused unchanged.

INTERPRETATION
Screening order by mass: 8, 16, 25, 2 ribs. Fine-sizing order: 2, 16, 25, 8 ribs. The previously excluded screening winner becomes the lightest fine-sized candidate. Its 24.53% screening disadvantage did not justify excluding it from fine sizing.
The 2-rib design has the greatest stress-sized mass but the smallest added reinforcement mass among these four outcomes. This describes the complete heuristic sizing path, not an isolated causal proof about rib count.
Future article revision must update the selected-candidate statement and candidate-promotion discussion. Existing 16-rib local and shell-solid validation and radius-sensitivity studies remain model-specific; they cannot be transferred automatically to the 2-rib result. No article edits were made.
